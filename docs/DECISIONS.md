# Decisions

Product decisions made by the owner. They override anything else in this repo. Add new decisions
here once they're made.

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name and address | **Where Countries Stand**, a separate website with its own domain (not a section or subdomain of the Parliament site, Where MPs Stand). A Where They Stand project, on the International shelf with Where Lives Stand. |
| 2 | Code | A new repo. Copy Where MPs Stand's engine (the site was then called Where They Stand) and adapt it. Pull out shared code into a common package later, once both sites work and the shared parts are obvious. |
| 3 | First version | General Debate, Security Council debates, General Assembly votes and group memberships, all from **1992**. General Debate and votes back to 1946 as an archive. Briefings default to the **last 10 years**, with an obvious control to widen the range. |
| 4 | Keeping current | The academic corpora for the back catalogue. Our own loader for UN verbatim records from 2024, checked against the corpora on overlapping years. |
| 5 | Groups | Countries are grouped by membership **on the date** (blocs, regions, income groups, Council membership), as Where MPs Stand uses party and role on the date. Before 1971, "China" is the Republic of China. |
| 6 | Country names | The UN's own names and status throughout (Member State, observer State, non-member), stated as a policy on the site. |
| 7 | Neutral wording | The charity's impartiality policy and Where MPs Stand's wording rules apply. Also not used in the site's own voice: "regime" (say "government"), "rogue state", "the West" and similar labels. "Condemned" is allowed when describing a formal statement. Group statements are attributed to the group, never to each member individually. |
| 8 | Licence | Code AGPL-3.0, as Where MPs Stand. Briefing text **CC BY-NC 4.0**, because of the UN's non-commercial terms. |
| 9 | Hosting and budget | Start on Where MPs Stand's server if there's room; otherwise a second server of about £40–70 a month. (It had room: the site runs on the charity's shared server, November 2026.) A separate AI spending cap, about $50 a month to start. |
| 10 | Testing | A test set of about 50 questions from the start, including sparse ones where "No records found" is the right answer. |
| 11 | Look | The look of the UN's printed Official Records, in a **single column** (not the records' two columns), with the UN's web palette and typeface for controls, and no UN name, emblem or wordmark. See [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md). |
| 12 | The General Debate website | Until a session's official records are out (about a year after the debate), statements come from the UN's General Debate website (gadebate.un.org). Its browser check blocks automated clients, so the update job presents itself as a browser, reading only what robots.txt allows, politely (October 2026). Texts from it are labelled as not yet the official record, and are replaced by it. |
| 13 | Rights of reply | Rights of reply in the General Debate are included as records of their own ("General debate: right of reply"), never mixed with the statements. Points of order are left out. Added from the UN's records from 1993 (the corpus has none; earlier records are unreadable scans). |
| 14 | Briefing length | Two kinds of briefing, as on Where MPs Stand: **Summary** (the default: 24 sources, about 300–450 words) and **Comprehensive** (40 sources, about 700–1,300 words, adding how positions changed over time and the main points of agreement and contention). The choice is shown openly beside the question box. |
| 15 | China and the Group of 77 | China is **not** counted as a member of the Group of 77: China says it is not a member, only a supporter, though the Group lists it and it contributes to the Group's funds. The site describes a country's memberships as the country itself states them. The Group's joint statements, made "on behalf of the Group of 77 and China", are attributed to the Group (decision 7). (October 2026) |
| 16 | Corrections and the right of reply | The charity's corrections and right-of-reply policy (impartiality policy, section 7, final for building on 12 November 2026) is built into the site, in the family's words (the charity's glossary). **An "If you're named" page** (`/named.html`, the address the charity's own right-of-reply page links to; in every page's footer as "If you're named", beside "Corrections", and linked from the corrections page as "If you're named on this site") for **countries' delegations and permanent missions to the UN and their governments**, **people named in the UN's records**, and anyone acting for them. It sets out the three routes, each with a way to start it on the corrections page's form: **"Something is wrong"** (`?kind=error`), **"You've been characterised unfairly"** (`?kind=unfair`) and **"Add a statement in your own words"** (`?kind=statement`, its own fields). `?kind=takedown` and `other` stay, and `problem`, the form's old value, is still accepted and means "Something is wrong". **Corrections cover the site's labels, groupings and connections, not only briefings:** the groups a country is counted in on the date (regions, regional groups, P5, elected members and invited non-members of the Council, AU and its suspensions, EU, G77, NAM, NATO, OIC, LDC, LLDC, SIDS, World Bank income groups) and how briefings group and balance by them, country names and status, speaker labels (who a statement is attributed to, roles, the Council's President, group statements, "(spoke in …)"), the "not yet the official record" and scanned-record labels, rights of reply kept apart, the counts above a briefing, and the links to sister sites. **Response times**, on the corrections page and the named page: "We reply within 5 working days, and decide within 20 working days." **Statements** (policy 7.2): up to 150 words, with the author's name and role, about one saved briefing (`/b/<id>`: the site's only items with an address of their own, as it has no country pages yet), an optional link to the record, and an email address to confirm who sent it. Held as waiting in `data/state.db` (table `statements`, beside the problem reports) until the owner has confirmed the sender, then published as written or declined with reasons; the author can have it updated or withdrawn at any time (`wcs statements`, `publish-statement`, `decline-statement`, `update-statement`, `withdraw-statement`; `docs/OPERATIONS.md`). Publishing needs the owner's note of how the sender was confirmed (`--checked`): it is kept in the statement's log, never shown, and should not hold the sender's email address. Shown after the briefing, headed "Statement from [name], [date]" and followed by "In their own words. Not checked or endorsed by Where Countries Stand.", in the controls' sans face on a dashed, sunk panel, apart from the record's serif. A published statement can be recorded as a country's (`--country`), so a country page can gather them once there is one (`GET /api/statements?country=`). **Never part of the record:** statements are never given to the AI model, never in the search index, and never in a briefing's saved or cached events; nothing in `wcs.rag`, `wcs.engine` or `wcs.index` reads them (`tests/test_statements.py`). The confirming address is kept while a statement is waiting or shown and **deleted three months after it is withdrawn or declined** (as each statement arrives, by every statement command, and twice a day by `wcs update`), as the privacy notice says. (November 2026) |
| 17 | Why use this rather than a chatbot? | The site says plainly why it is worth using when the same question can be put to a general AI chatbot, in the family's shared wording (the charity's glossary, "Why use this rather than a chatbot?"; phase 1b of the shared-engine brief). **On the home page**, straight under the question box, without moving it: one line that is always shown, "Answers only from the UN's records, every point linked and checked.", and a disclosure (a plain `<details>`, no script) headed "Why use this rather than a chatbot?" with five one-line points (only the record; every point linked and checked; up to date; it says when there is nothing; balanced by design) and the line "Briefings here are written by AI too. The difference is what the AI is given and how it is checked.", linking to the About page's "How a briefing is made" (the site has no methodology page of its own). It goes with the rest of the home page when a briefing is shown. **On the About page**, a section of the same name with the same points at more length and two more (records searched in full; open), linking to the page's own sections instead of repeating them. **Where this site's words differ from the family's, because the family's would not be true here:** "the UN's records", not "the official record", in the line that is always shown, because until a General Debate's verbatim record is published its speeches come from the UN's website (the delegation's prepared text, or an AI transcript of the interpretation), labelled "not yet the official record" (decision 12), and both pages say so; evidence is chosen across "groups of countries (by region, by bloc or by place at the Security Council)", one grouping to a briefing, not "regions and blocs"; and document symbols are claimed only for statements taken from the UN's verbatim records, since General Debate statements from the corpus have none. Every claim was checked against the code and settings on 13 November 2026 and must be checked again before it is changed: what the model is given (`wcs.rag.answer`, rule 1 of `wcs.rag.prompts`), the checks (`wcs.rag.checks`, which sets up the shared package's), "No records found" (`wcs.rag.pipeline`), the balance (`wcs.rank.evidence`), the twice-daily update (`deploy/wcs-update.timer`), coverage (`config/settings.toml`, `DATA.md`) and the privacy notice. Opens of the disclosure are not counted; a count, with no reader tracking, may come with the per-briefing statistics. (November 2026) |
| 18 | The shared engine | The site is built on **wts-core**, the Where They Stand sites' shared package (public, AGPL-3.0, taken as the wheel of a release at a fixed version, the same tag the CI workflows are called at), in place of its own copies of what the four sites did alike: settings, the state database, limits and spending, the checks on briefings, the flow of a question, the model client, the embedder and the index's tables, reports and the right of reply, backups, and the evaluation harness. The site keeps what follows the UN's record (loaders, attribution, groups, the index's columns, reading a question, retrieval and ranking, the instructions, the pages) and every word a reader is shown. **Readers see no change** but those the package's design lists: a written answer carries the version of the checking rules that wrote it; a report of nothing but spaces is refused; a sender with two faults may be told of the other first; a statement the site takes down is "removed", not "withdrawn"; a statement nobody decides in three months lapses. Each is asserted in `tests/test_parity.py`, which compares the site with what its own code gave before (`tests/golden/`). **New:** one record for each briefing (time, cost, what the checks found, nothing about the reader; 13 months), a weekly report and alert thresholds the owner sets, hiding a briefing or a record while a concern is decided, the corrections page able to carry what is said publicly of a correction, and a restore that refuses a sister site's backup. The "note about the evidence" rule stays the wider of the family's two forms, as it was: narrowing it is a change of its own, to be measured first. The old tables in `data/state.db` are left as they were and dropped in a later release (`docs/OPERATIONS.md`, "The state database"). (November 2026) |
