About Where Countries Stand

Where Countries Stand prepares short, balanced briefings on what countries have said at the United Nations, and how they have voted.

AI briefings

Briefings are written by AI from the UN’s records only, and every citation is checked. Each one is labelled as written by AI. The methodology page sets out what the model is given and how each briefing is checked, in full.

What the model is given

The AI is given only the UN records found for your question and may use nothing else: statements in the Security Council and the General Assembly’s General Debate, and the General Assembly’s recorded votes. Evidence is drawn from across groups of countries (by region, by bloc or by place at the Security Council).

How it is checked

Each point links to its source. Citations are checked against the source text, quotations are checked word for word, and sentences that no source supports are flagged. If the record holds nothing relevant, the site says “No records found”. The checks catch many errors but not all.

More about the site

  • Status shows whether briefings are being written and the latest record from each source.

In the family

Where Countries Stand is a Where They Stand project. Its sister sites are Where MPs Stand, Where Councils Stand, Where Landlords Stand and Where Lives Stand; the charity’s own site is wheretheystand.uk.
