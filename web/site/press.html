Press and contact

Where Countries Stand writes sourced, impartial briefings on what countries have said, and how they have voted, at the United Nations. Where Countries Stand is independent. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the United Nations, and it does not use the UN’s name or emblem.

Contact

  • Email admin@wherecountriesstand.com for press, research or anything else.
  • To report a mistake in a briefing, please use the report form, so that it is logged and any correction published.

How to cite

Briefings are written by AI from the UN’s records, so where you can, quote and cite the record itself. Every source under a briefing gives the UN’s document symbol, the meeting and the date, and links to the official record. To cite a statement:

[Country], statement to the United Nations Security Council, [number]th meeting, [date]. S/PV.[number]. https://undocs.org/en/S/PV.[number]

[Country], statement to the United Nations General Assembly, [session]th session, [number]th plenary meeting, [date]. A/[session]/PV.[number]. https://undocs.org/en/A/[session]/PV.[number]

A General Assembly vote is cited by its resolution, such as A/RES/80/1, with the date of the vote.

To cite a briefing itself, give its address, which is permanent, and the date it was written, which is shown under it:

Where Countries Stand (2026). “How have small island States spoken about climate change since 2015?” Briefing written by AI from the United Nations record, 6 November 2026. https://wherecountriesstand.com/b/… (accessed 6 November 2026). CC BY-NC 4.0.

The site in brief

  • Covers every country’s statement in the General Assembly’s annual General Debate, Security Council debates, and every recorded vote in the General Assembly. The record, as of today:
  • General Debatefrom 1946
  • Security Council debatesfrom 1992
  • General Assembly votesfrom 1946
  • The UN’s records are checked for new meetings twice a day; the status page shows the latest record from each source.
  • Every point in a briefing quotes the UN’s own verbatim record and links to it, and is checked against it word for word; see About.
  • Statements made in other languages are the UN’s English translations, as printed in its records.
  • A Where They Stand project. Where They Stand is applying to register as a charity in England and Wales; until it is registered, the site is run by its founder, Rob Mitchell. No advertising, no tracking cookies, and no account needed.
