Glossary

The terms from the United Nations that briefings and their filters use. Groups are counted by membership on the date of each statement, so a country is in a group only for the years it belonged to it.

Groups of countries

P5: the permanent members of the Security Council
China, France, the Russian Federation, the United Kingdom and the United States, named in the UN Charter. The Russian Federation holds the seat of the USSR from 24 December 1991; before 25 October 1971 China’s seat was held by the Republic of China.
Elected members of the Security Council
The ten members elected by the General Assembly for two years at a time, as listed in each meeting’s record.
Non-members invited to speak
Countries that are not on the Security Council but are invited to take part in a meeting, under rules 37 and 39 of its provisional rules of procedure.
SIDS: small island developing States
The UN’s list of small island countries and territories that share particular challenges, such as rising seas and remoteness. The site uses the UN Statistics Division’s current list.
LDC: least developed countries
Low-income countries facing severe obstacles to development, on a list the UN reviews every three years. The site uses the current list.
LLDC: landlocked developing countries
Developing countries with no coastline, on the UN’s current list.
G77: the Group of 77
The largest group of developing countries at the UN, founded in 1964 by 77 countries and now about 130. Its joint statements are made “on behalf of the Group of 77 and China”. China says it is not a member, only a supporter, so the site does not count it as one; the Group’s statements are attributed to the Group.
NAM: the Non-Aligned Movement
Countries that chose not to join either side in the Cold War, founded in 1961, with about 120 members.
OIC: the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation
57 states with large Muslim populations, founded in 1969. Its suspensions of members (Egypt, Afghanistan, Syria) are counted for the years they lasted.
AU: the African Union
The countries of Africa, as the Organization of African Unity before 2002. Morocco is counted from 30 January 2017. A country suspended from the Union’s activities is still a member.
The UN’s regional groups
Five groups used to share out seats in UN bodies: the African Group, the Asia-Pacific Group, the Eastern European Group, the Latin American and Caribbean Group, and the Western European and other States.
Regions
Africa, the Americas, Asia, Europe and Oceania, as the UN Statistics Division divides the world for its statistics (the M49 standard). These are geographic, unlike the regional groups.
Member State and observer State
Countries are named as the UN names them. The Holy See and the State of Palestine are observer States: they take part in the General Assembly but do not vote.
Group statement
A statement made on behalf of a group (“I have the honour to speak on behalf of the African Group”). It is shown as the group’s position, never as each member’s.

The record

Verbatim record
The UN’s official written account of a meeting, with every statement in full. Statements made in other languages are printed in the UN’s English translation; the site marks them “(spoke in French)” and so on, as the record does.
Provisional record
A verbatim record before delegations’ corrections are made. It is replaced by the final version.
Document symbol
The reference the UN gives each document, used in citations so that it can be found. S/PV.7565 is the record of the Security Council’s 7,565th meeting. A/80/PV.5 is the record of the fifth plenary meeting of the General Assembly’s 80th session. S/RES/2334 (2016) and A/RES/80/1 are resolutions of the Council and the Assembly.
Session
The General Assembly’s year, from September to September. The first session was in 1946; the 80th began in September 2025.
General Debate
The opening of each session of the General Assembly, at which each country’s leader or minister speaks on any subject. Until a session’s verbatim record is published, about a year later, the site uses the texts on the UN’s General Debate website and marks them as not yet the official record.
Right of reply
A short answer by one country to what another said in a debate, given at the end of the day’s meeting. The site keeps rights of reply apart from the statements they answer.
Recorded vote
A vote in which each country’s vote is listed: in favour, against or abstaining. Countries that are present but do not vote, or absent, are listed as not voting.
President of the Security Council
The member that chairs the Council for a month, in turn. When presiding, its representative speaks for the Council; the site counts only what they say “in my capacity as the representative of” their country as the country’s.
