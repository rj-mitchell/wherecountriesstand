Corrections and problems

Briefings are written by an AI model from the UN’s own records, and every point is checked against its source, but mistakes can still get through. If something is wrong, misleading or unfair, please tell us. A briefing found to be wrong is withdrawn: its address then says so and why, and it is listed below.

We reply within 5 working days, and decide within 20 working days. Requests to remove something about a person, and anything that may be unlawful or harmful, are dealt with sooner.

Corrections cover everything the site says, not only briefings: the groups a country is counted in on the date (regions, the UN’s regional groups, its place at the Security Council, blocs such as the African Union or the Group of 77, and income groups), country names and status, who a statement is attributed to and the speaker’s role, statements made on behalf of a group, the labels saying a text is a translation or not yet the official record, the counts above a briefing, and the links it makes to sister sites. Mistakes in the UN’s records themselves can only be corrected by the United Nations.

If you speak for a country at the United Nations, or you are named in its records, If you’re named on this site explains the three routes (something is wrong, you’ve been characterised unfairly, or adding a statement in your own words), how we check who you are, and how to appeal a decision.

Report a problem

What is it about?

A fact, a quotation, a date, a label, a group or a connection is incorrect, or a briefing misstates the record.

Your report

The address is used only to reply to you, and deleted when the report is dealt with (see the privacy notice).

Your statement

A statement is shown beside the briefing it is about, as you wrote it, once we have confirmed who sent it. It can be up to 150 words, in your own name; it can’t be defamatory or abusive, be about a private individual or another person’s conduct, or appeal for votes, money or support. See If you’re named on this site.
