Privacy notice

Where Countries Stand sets no cookies and measures nothing unless you agree, never shows advertising, and you do not need an account to use it. This notice explains the little it does store, and why.

Last updated 22 November 2026 (draft, for when measurement is turned on).

Data controller

Where Countries Stand is a Where They Stand project. Where They Stand, a charitable incorporated organisation registered in England and Wales (charity number NNNNNNN), is the data controller. Contact: admin@wherecountriesstand.com.

Measuring visits for Google’s advertising grant

Where They Stand advertises its sites on Google Search with Google Ad Grants, free advertising that Google gives charities. To keep the grant, it has to show Google that the ads lead people to use the sites. So, only if you agree, this site uses the Google tag to tell Google when you finish reading a briefing, open a source, follow the Donate link or send a report or a statement. That is all it tells Google about: no list of the pages you read, and nothing you type in a report or a statement.

Nothing is loaded from Google until you choose. A box at the top of the page asks you, with “Accept” and “Reject”. If you reject, or don’t choose, nothing is loaded from Google and no cookie is set. If you accept, your browser loads the Google tag from Google, which sets cookies whose names start “_gcl_” on this site, and sends Google your IP address, details of your browser, the address of the page and the action. The address is sent without anything after its “?” or “#”, so a question you asked, which can be in a page’s address, is never sent to Google. Google uses these details to count how many visits from its ads led to that action.

Google receives this information as an independent controller, for its own purposes, under the Google Ads Controller-Controller Data Protection Terms. How it uses it is set out in Google’s privacy policy and on its Business Data Responsibility page. Google says its “_gcl_” cookies last for 90 days (how Google uses cookies), and explains how long it keeps what it collects in how Google retains data.

The legal basis is your consent. We keep only your choice, and the day you made it, in your own browser (in its local storage, not in a cookie), and we never receive it. After a year you are asked again.

To change your mind, use “Measurement choices” at the foot of any page. Choosing “Reject” after accepting deletes Google’s cookies on this site and reloads the page with nothing from Google. Clearing this site’s data in your browser also removes them, and your choice.

No ads are ever shown on this site or on the charity’s other sites, and nothing on them is personalised. The Google tag is told not to use your visit to personalise ads.

Information stored

Your questions and the briefings written for them

Each briefing is saved with its question at its own address, so that it can be shared, so that the same question can be answered again without paying for a new briefing, and so that a complaint about a briefing can be checked against exactly what was shown. Saved briefings are not listed anywhere and are not linked to you. If a briefing cannot be written, the question and the error are noted, so that the fault can be found; these notes are deleted after 90 days. Please do not put personal details in a question.

For each briefing the site also notes how long each step took, what it cost and what the automatic checks found, so that slow or poorly checked briefings can be found and fixed. These notes hold nothing that identifies you; they are kept for 13 months, and the question of a briefing that could not be written is removed from them after 90 days.

Problem reports

If you report a problem, your message is saved, with your email address if you choose to give one. The email address is used only to reply to you, and is deleted when the report is dealt with. If a briefing is withdrawn, its question and the reason are listed on the corrections page; your name and address never are. If you agreed to measurement, Google is told only that a report or a statement was sent, never what it says or who sent it.

Statements under the right of reply

A country’s delegation, permanent mission or government, or a person named in the UN’s records (or someone acting for them), can add a short statement beside what this site says about them (see If you’re named). The statement is published with their name and role, as they wrote it, and is never sent to the AI model. The email address used to confirm who they are, and to let them update or withdraw it, is kept while the statement is waiting or shown, and deleted three months after it is withdrawn, declined or removed.

Backups

Backups of the site’s database are kept for up to two months, so a deleted address can remain in a backup until then. The backup made each night, at 4.20am UTC, also holds the times of the briefings asked since midnight, until it is deleted, but no visitor codes and not the secret they are made with (see below).

A daily visitor code in place of your IP address

To apply the daily limit on new briefings and prevent abuse, your IP address is turned into a scrambled code, which is kept with the time and cost of each briefing you ask for until the end of that day (UTC). After that day the code and the time of day are removed: only the day, the cost and how much text was sent and written are kept, with nothing that says who asked. The secret used to make the code changes every day and the old one is deleted, so a code cannot be linked to the next day’s or turned back into an address. Your IP address itself is not stored.

Settings on your device

The briefing length you choose is part of the page’s address, not stored by us. Your choice about measurement is kept in your own browser (above).

Information about people in the record

The site republishes the United Nations’ public records, which name the people who spoke: heads of State and Government, ministers, diplomats and UN officials. Briefings describe them by their public role, as the record does. This is processed in the public interest of reporting on what countries say at the United Nations. The record itself can only be corrected by the United Nations. If you think the site gets something wrong about you, or you would like to add a statement in your own words, see If you’re named on this site.

Other organisations

  • Anthropic writes the briefings. Your question, and the UN records found for it, are sent to Anthropic’s API to read the question and to write each briefing. Under Anthropic’s commercial terms, this is not used to train its models. See Anthropic’s privacy policy.
  • Cloudflare carries visits to wherecountriesstand.com to the server, to shield the site from floods of automated requests. It sees each request as it passes, including your IP address, possibly outside the UK, under Cloudflare’s privacy policy. Its analytics and visitor tracking are turned off for this site. If Cloudflare ever has to check that a visitor is not an automated program (during an attack, say), it may set a short-lived security cookie of its own for that purpose only.
  • The hosting provider runs the server and has no access to the site’s data beyond keeping it running.

Nothing is sold or shared for marketing. The site loads no third-party scripts, fonts or trackers, except the Google tag if you agree to measurement (above).

Legal basis and how long it is kept

Information is processed on the basis of legitimate interests: running a free public service, keeping its costs within budget, preventing abuse, and correcting errors. Measurement for Google’s advertising grant is on the basis of your consent (above). Saved briefings are kept while the site runs, unless withdrawn. Report contact details are deleted when the report is closed. The email address given with a statement is kept while it is waiting or shown, and deleted three months after it is withdrawn or declined. Notes of failed briefings are deleted after 90 days. Visitor codes, and the time of day of each briefing’s cost, are removed after the day they are made. Backups hold no visitor codes; the night’s backup keeps the times of the hours after midnight until it is deleted. Server error logs are kept briefly, and requests are not logged.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask for a copy of information held about you, and ask for it to be corrected or deleted, object to its use, and withdraw your consent to measurement at any time, as above. Because the site does not know who you are, it may need your help to find it, such as the question you asked and when. Email admin@wherecountriesstand.com.

If you are unhappy with the answer, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.
